# Launch Playbook

A launch is product-ready × audience-ready. Most launches fail on the second,
not the first. Build the audience before the date, not on it.

## Readiness gate (all green before the date)

- Product works end to end, tested on the real stack.
- Pricing live in every currency you sell in; the buy path works for a
  signed-out stranger, not just you.
- The promo code exists, is dated to the launch window, and covers **every**
  product in the launch (check the applies-to list — a missing SKU gets no
  discount).
- The store flips from "coming soon" to in-stock on the date (schedule it;
  don't rely on a manual flip at midnight).
- Analytics/purchase tracking fires, so you can see what sold.

## Build the audience first

- A countdown with a **notify-me** box collects intent before launch — but only
  if traffic reaches it. An empty notify list on launch morning means no one is
  watching. Drive eyes to the page in the weeks before (content, SEO, the
  places your buyers already are).
- Email the notify list **on the day**, with the code.
- A one-time announcement to existing customers — but segment it: send to the
  subset who actually fit the product, not the whole list.

## Honest demand math

Work backwards from the target, in units:

```
extra revenue needed  ÷  average sale price  =  sales needed
sales needed  ÷  realistic conversion of reachable audience  =  audience needed
```

If the audience needed is far larger than what you can reach by the date, the
launch is a *spike*, not a plan — say so, and separate the one-time spike from
sustained recurring revenue. Don't dress a hopeful number as a forecast.

## Channels (earned, not spammed)

- Post where your buyers gather, as a genuine participant, not a blast. For a
  developer/operator product that means the communities and searches they use.
- Never mass-cold-email strangers or astroturf. It burns the brand and the
  domain's deliverability for a short bump.
- Prepare the assets (announcement, email, offer page) so posting is one paste;
  the human decides where.

## After launch

Report sales **and** the funnel (visits → notify signups → orders), not vanity
traffic. Keep what converted, cut what didn't, and turn one-time buyers into
recurring where the product allows (annual, subscription).
