# Support Playbook

A good reply ends the customer's problem, not the ticket. Lead with the answer.

## The shape of a reply

1. **Answer the actual question first.** Not "thank you for contacting us" —
   the answer. If they asked how to add a domain, the first line is how.
2. **Ground it in their account.** Pull the verified facts (service status,
   invoice, domain, last ticket) and use them. "Invoice #903 for ₹69.40 is
   unpaid, due 19 Nov" beats "please check your invoices."
3. **One clear next step.** Exactly what they do next, and what happens after.
4. **A human path.** How to reach a person if this doesn't land.
5. **Sign off as the brand's support,** warm and plain.

## Hard rules

- **Never invent facts.** No made-up prices, limits, uptime figures, dates, or
  features. If you don't have a verified answer, say so and route it to a human
  — a confident wrong answer is worse than "let me check."
- **Promise nothing you can't keep.** No guaranteed timelines you don't
  control, no "it'll be fixed today" unless it will.
- **Don't advertise internal targets** (e.g. an internal first-response goal is
  not a customer promise).
- **Match the channel.** Short and skimmable; steps as numbered steps, not a
  wall of grey text.

## Tone

Plain, specific, no filler. Drop the AI tells: no "it's not X, it's Y", no
"rest assured", no exclamation-mark spray, no "I hope this email finds you
well." Write like a competent human who respects the reader's time.

## When you can't answer

Say what you checked, what you found, and that a person will take it from here
— then actually escalate it. "I don't have a verified answer for that, so I've
passed it to the team who can confirm" is a complete, honest reply.

## Verify before sending

- The account facts you quoted are this customer's, not another's.
- Every claim is supportable.
- The next step actually works (the link, the button, the path exist).
- No internal notes, other customers' data, or secrets leaked into the reply.
