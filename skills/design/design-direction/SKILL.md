# Design Direction

Instantiate the philosophy in root `DESIGN.md` into a concrete, binding
visual language for one project. Output: the project's `DESIGN.md` (start
from `templates/project/DESIGN.md.template`).

## Procedure

1. **Inputs**: product brief (audience, positioning, differentiation),
   research packet (references, category patterns), any existing brand.
2. **Direction statement** (3–5 sentences): the personality and point of
   view. Must be falsifiable — "clean and modern" describes everything and
   thus nothing. "Editorial confidence: oversized serif headlines, narrow
   measure, ink-on-paper palette, one electric accent" is a direction.
3. **Typography**: families (max 2 + optional mono) *with reasons*; the
   modular scale (base, ratio, named steps); weight/case/spacing usage
   rules per level.
4. **Color**: palette with roles (dominant/supporting/accent/semantic/
   neutrals), usage proportions, contrast-checked text pairs, dark-mode
   stance (designed or explicitly out of scope).
5. **Spacing**: base unit, scale steps, section-rhythm rules.
6. **Shape & depth**: radius language, border vs. shadow philosophy,
   elevation levels if any.
7. **Motion language**: with motion-director — duration bands, easing set,
   what may move, what never moves.
8. **Imagery direction**: with media-director — subject matter, mood,
   composition, treatment; what's photographed vs. illustrated vs. code.
9. **Voice**: tone, sentence length, person, words we use/never use.
10. **Anti-slop stance**: which category clichés this project must
    specifically avoid (from competitor research).

## Quality bar

- Another designer could produce on-language work from the doc alone.
- Every choice has a reason tied to audience/brand/job — "it looks nice"
  is not a reason.
- The direction differentiates: put the doc next to three competitors'
  sites; if it could describe them too, sharpen it.

## Maintenance

The doc is law during build; changes go through design-director with a
recorded rationale, and downstream work re-checks against the new language.
